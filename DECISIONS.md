# Decisions

One line each: what we chose and why.

- New project `Kabelmeter` in this repo instead of the earlier "installatieplanner" prototype: the brief and plan describe the Kabelmeter. The old prototype stays in `~/Documents/Untitled Project`.
- Kept the signing team and bundle id that were set for the first device run, so the installed profile keeps working.
- Deployment target iOS 26.0 instead of the Xcode 27 default: the test iPhone runs iOS 26.4.
- Pure logic in `Kabelmeter/Core`, also exposed as a Swift package: no iOS simulator runtime is installed, so `swift test` on the Mac runs the unit tests.
- `fixedLengths` uses an exact search for the cheapest roll combination (fewest rolls on a tie): the "largest rolls + smallest for the rest" rule ordered up to 45 m too much (1152 m → 1200 m instead of 1155 m).
- Floating point tolerance of 1e-6 m before rounding up, so 10.000000001 m is billed as 10 m.
- Raycast from the screen center 15 times per second, `.existingPlaneGeometry` first, then `.estimatedPlane`; the "+" button is disabled without a hit.
- "Te weinig licht" when ARKit's ambient intensity is below 150 lumen.
- Segments are thin boxes scaled along their length; no per-segment labels (the total in the price bar is what matters).
- Cursor is averaged over the last 5 hits (a jump over 10 cm is followed at once) and held for 0.5 s when ARKit loses the surface: less jitter, "+" stays usable.
- Raycast falls back to detected vertical planes extended infinitely, so a wall ARKit saw only partly still gives a hit. Risk: past a corner the extended plane can give a wrong hit.
- Segments within 8° of vertical or level snap to it ("Verticaal" / "Waterpas"). Without a surface hit, aiming within 2.5° of the vertical line through the last point measures along that line, like Apple's Measure app.
- LiDAR (Pro models) is used automatically through scene reconstruction and estimated-plane raycasts; no separate code path.
- Cable data from the elektramat.nl product pages (schema.org data) at Jairo's request, instead of sample data: the database export had not arrived. Waits 10 s between requests, per robots.txt.
- Only "per meter" products (whole meters, minimum 1, as the shop's quantity field allows). Rolls and reels are separate products with their own price, so `fixedLengths` is not used yet.
- Price per meter including VAT: the app shows a consumer price.
- Photos ship in the bundle as `<sku>.jpg` (JPEG, 300 px, 164 KB in total) instead of loading from the site: the demo must work without Wi-Fi.
- Switch and socket data comes from `assets/sku_mapping.csv` + `sku_parts.csv` via `scripts/fixtures_to_json.py` into one `fixtures.json`; the app does not parse CSV.
- The 8 carat rows name models that do not exist (frame material + insert colour, e.g. `witglas-studiowit`); they use the model with the same insert colour (`carat_studiowit`). The frame material is not shown in 3D.
- Fixtures that are not completely orderable (5 of 40) can still be placed, marked "Niet compleet te bestellen" and left out of the total.
- Each placement is an `ARAnchor` named `fixture:<id>`: ARKit keeps it fixed in the room. The orientation is computed from the wall normal (+Z out of the wall, +Y up), so switches always hang upright.
- No Elektramat MCP cart: without a login `cable_create_order` returns only the generic cart URL (no cart id or session), so Safari would show an empty cart. Details in `RemoteCartService`.
- Web app in `webapp/` without a build step (plain ES modules), with the pricing ported 1:1 and the same test cases; data is copied from the app by `scripts/sync_webapp_data.sh`.
- Web AR uses WebXR (Android Chrome only; iPhone Safari has none). On iPhone the web app offers manual route input and AR Quick Look for the USDZ switches. three.js is loaded from a pinned CDN URL only when AR starts.
- WebXR placements are simple 3D frames (sizes from the model manifest) instead of the USDZ models: three.js cannot reliably load these USDZ files.
- The USDZ models keep their `preliminary:anchoring` (AR Quick Look needs it to snap to walls); the app strips the resulting AnchoringComponent after loading, because an anchor nested in our own AnchorEntity was not drawn. A model that fails to load shows as a plain white frame instead of nothing.
- Product previews are PNGs rendered offline from the models (`scripts/render_fixture_previews.py`, reusing `preview.py`), 936 KB for 38 images, instead of rendering thumbnails on the device: same images in the app and the web app, and they can be checked without a device.
- Cables and fixtures click together: every placed fixture has four connection points (middle of each frame edge). The measuring crosshair snaps to one within 6 cm, before vertical/level snapping. A fixture being placed snaps with an edge onto a cable point, or edge-to-opposite-edge next to a fixture on the same wall (taking over its rotation, so frames line up).
- Height above the floor is shown as a dotted line plus a label under the crosshair. The floor is the lowest horizontal plane ARKit classifies as floor, otherwise the lowest one at least 0.8 m below the camera; without a floor the app asks to aim at it instead of guessing. Fixture height is measured to the middle of the frame.
- The web app (`webapp/`) does not have these features yet.
- Auto mode draws cables the way an installer routes them: straight up to the horizontal zone at 2.25 m above the floor (or 0.3 m above the highest point without a known floor), along the wall, through the corner when the next fixture is on another wall, and down to the top edge of the fixture. Fixtures are chained nearest-first from the start point ("+" on the electrical cabinet) or, after adding, from the last fixture that has a cable. Price uses the same reserve and 2 m connection allowance per cable.
- The app is organised in rooms ("Mijn ruimtes"); a room is one design (JSON in Documents/Projects, saved after every change). Inside a room: "Bekijken" (no crosshair, tap a part or cable for an info card) and "Bewerken" with two steps: 1 Onderdelen, then 2 Kabels (Automatisch or Handmatig). This replaces the earlier four-mode bar, which mixed looking, placing and measuring.
- One shared cart, worked out from all rooms (`Bill`) instead of a separate list with "add to cart" buttons: it always matches the designs and cannot be added twice. Cable: per cable type the required length of every cable (reserve + 2 m connection per cable) is added up over all rooms and rounded once to how it is sold. Parts: one per placed part, by Elektramat code. The cart button with the total is visible on every screen.
- Each room saves its own ARWorldMap next to its JSON (at most every 3 s while mapped, on leaving and when the app goes to the background), never while the room is still being recognised. Opening a room first lets ARKit recognise it; "Niet herkend? Toon hier" shows the design relative to where the user stands. The old single `placements.worldmap` is no longer used.
- Tapping finds the nearest part (within 44 pt of its middle on screen) or cable (within 28 pt of a segment), projected with `ARView.project`; the info card shows product, Elektramat codes, height, length, order length and price, plus Verwijder/Wissel/Verplaats in "Bewerken".
- `CartService` / `CartStore` were removed with the separate cart; the open questions about a real webshop cart moved to the comment on `Bill`.
- No Demo-modus any more: it mixed a fake wall with real rooms and confused the flow. The Simulator (no camera) still shows a drawn wall so the UI can be checked without a device; on a device the app always uses the camera.
- Placing parts: the part to place is chosen once (bottom card, "Wijzig": switch or socket, then per brand) and shown as a see-through preview on the wall; every "+" places it right away. Faster than asking after each "+", and you see what you get before you tap.
- While placing, the preview lines up with the parts already on the wall (level, or straight above/below) or with the standard height for its kind (1,05 m switch, 0,30 m socket, NL practice), shown as cyan dotted guide lines. Only within 4 cm, so placing a part half a meter higher on purpose just works. Clicking onto a cable end or next to another part (edge to edge) comes first.
- Handmatig cables default to right angles: from the last anchor first straight up or down, then sideways to the crosshair, the way cable runs along a wall; "+" adds the corner and the point together and "Ongedaan" removes both. A toggle allows straight lines. "Klaar" ends the cable.
- Every placed part and every finished cable is confirmed with a green checkmark toast, a success haptic and a 1 s highlight of what was just added, so it is clear when something really counts.
- "Verplaats" takes the part off the wall; the next "+" puts it back, "Annuleer" (or leaving the parts step) puts it back where it was, so nothing is lost by accident.
